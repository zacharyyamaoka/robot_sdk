Joint controllers: composed boxes over one bus

Composed boxes, one writer by wiring. The JTC, the Joint Command Controller and the collision detector are peers. A small controller manager (already on robot_msgs main) is the only box wired to the bus. The five-bar mapping goes up into the robot model, so every controller works in servo space. Below the manager, one thin Arm (a role bound by a joint table) turns joint names into CAN ids, signs, offsets and the homed zero.
ServoCommand.mode landed (robot_msgs 3eea5a0). The JCC's Follow now goes to the peer JTC by wire (robot_msgs 8c3e6ed). The big step left is moving the rig runners off the bus and onto the manager.

The stack in one picture

  • Parts are done once they exist: a datasheet, a driver, a twin (robot_parts).
  • Bus box: the one place the robot's I/O lives. ServoCommand in, ServoState out, every tick.
  • Controllers: boxes that tick constantly. The JTC, the Joint Command Controller (JCC) and the collision detector sit side by side, wired, not nested.
  • Manager: the only box wired to the bus. It merges per joint by claim and priority, and its stop always wins.
  • Callers: everything we build from now on. They depend on two message types, not on a robot.

Composition, not nesting (your correction, adopted)

Agreed. And main is already halfway there. A peer session landed two things on robot_msgs today: JointCommandController (55ddc46, S011's primitives, ruler v2 0/45) and a ControllerManager (df7fd6b, ros2_control's shape). The manager makes the JTC and the JCC peers. It owns the bus, gives each controller the joints its goal claims, refuses a lower-priority steal, merges per joint, and its stop() always wins. That's your side-by-side picture, with the merge kept in its own box:

The one nesting left to remove (now done, robot_msgs 8c3e6ed, as follow(trajectory, via="jtc")). On main, the JCC's Follow step still builds the JTC inside itself through a follower factory (joint_command_controller.py:28, _Follow at :276). In the composed form, Follow becomes two primitives the JCC already has:

# today (nested): the JTC lives inside the JCC's Follow step
follow(trajectory)             # -> _Follow(make=JointTrajectoryController) ticked by the JCC

# composed: the JCC asks the JTC box, then waits on its status wire
[Command(send_to="jtc", trajectory=trajectory),
 Condition(until=jtc_status_is("done"), fail=jtc_status_is("aborted"), timeout=trajectory.duration + 0.5)]

Why a separate manager, not "the JCC is the mux": if the JCC were also the one writer, it would be a box with two jobs (running recipes and arbitrating the bus), and the JTC would always be downstream of it. With the manager as the mux, the JCC and JTC are symmetric peers. A cell with no recipes is just JTC → manager → bus, and the manager is ~1 small file with one rule set (claims, priority, stop wins). This reverses my earlier "you don't need a manager". You were half right: you don't need a big one, and the one on main is small.

The cost: a wire hop between JCC, JTC and manager. like_keras places a System in one process at one rate (dep.place(cell.arm, process="controller", hz=...)), so the boxes tick in order in one step, with no extra lag if those wires aren't lagged.

Precedent already running: like_keras's worked example has the same shape, with a guard in the manager's seat:

# like_keras/examples/pick_cell/cell.py today
with lk.System("arm_controller") as s:
    io, jtc, guard = Arm(), JtcPy(), GuardPy()
    jtc(state=io.state)
    guard(state=io.state, proposed=jtc.command)
    io(command=guard.safe)                       # one writer: the guard

# proposed
with lk.System("arm_controller") as s:
    io, hit = Arm(), CollisionDetector()
    jtc, jcc, mux = JointTrajectoryController(), JointCommandController(), ControllerManager()
    hit(state=io.state, reference=jtc.command)                      # read-only
    jtc(state=io.state, trajectory=jcc.follow, contact=hit.contact)
    jcc(state=io.state, jtc_status=jtc.status, contact=hit.contact)
    mux(state=io.state, jcc=jcc.command, jtc=jtc.command)           # claims, priority, stop wins
    io(command=mux.command)                                         # one writer: the manager
    s.expose(jcc.goal, jcc.result, jtc.status, io.state, hit.contact, stop=mux.stop)

Open detail: callers' own trajectories and the JCC's follow both target the JTC's one trajectory input. I'd route caller trajectories through the manager's send_goal("jtc", trajectory) (it already does replace_input), so the JTC still has one source.

From robot parts to an Arm: 5 API proposals

Your note, sharpened: yes, put one thin Arm between the controllers and the bus. And there's a sharper reason than "it's tidy". A bought arm (a UR5e, an xArm, everything in robotparts.arm) already is this box: you send it joint names and positions and never see a servo. Our arms are the only ones where CAN ids leak upward (the JCC track's cases name joints can11, and bam_fivebar/reachable.py:43 hard-codes {"j2": {"can_id": 12}, "c1": {"can_id": 13}}). The Arm makes our servo arms look like a bought arm.

What the Arm does, exactly, so it stays light. It does the 1:1 per-servo things from the transmission rule, and nothing else:

  • name ↔ servo address (bus, CAN id): the thing you want gone from commands
  • sign, offset and the homed zero: your "what if you want to apply a joint offset or a zero?" case. The homed map is runtime state, which the Arm reads after homing.
  • which transport (fdcanusb, pi3hat, twin): your "if I change from CAN USB I shouldn't need to change my code"
  • Not the five-bar. The Arm's joints are the servo joints (j2, c1), and the coupling stays in the robot model (decision B above).

Scores are 1-3 per criterion, multiplied by its weight. They're my judgement, made explicit so you can disagree with one cell.

Pick (my default, easy to switch): P1, with P4 falling out for free. The servo-level Arm role is the thin box, bound by a joint table. The System that wraps Arm + manager + JTC + JCC is what a planner talks to. like_keras's pick_cell already calls it arm_controller(). A UR5e binds at that System level (its cabinet is the JTC). Our servo arms bind at the role level. Both have the same outside: trajectory, goal, state, result, stop. P2's debug path is kept as a method on the REAL binding (ServoArm.servo(12)), not as a box.

Prior art: ros2_control's <ros2_control type="system"> hardware component, which lists joints and puts per-joint params (CAN id, sign) in the robot description, is P1. Viam's arm component (joint positions in, no motors visible) next to its motor components is the P1 + P4 split.
